# E-commerce Sales Funnel Analysis (SQL)

A SQL project on a quick-commerce grocery store. It follows the customer from the
first app visit to the final delivery, and studies the three things that break the
funnel after the order is already placed:

1. **Cancellations** — who cancels, why, and how much money it costs
2. **Substitutions** — what happens when an item is out of stock and a swap is offered
3. **Product role** — which products pull traffic, which ones convert, which ones quietly leak revenue

All queries use only basic SQL: `SELECT`, `JOIN`, `GROUP BY`, `CASE WHEN`, `COUNT`,
`SUM`, `ROUND`, `ORDER BY`, `LIMIT`. No window functions, no CTEs, no stored procedures.

---

## Dataset

Synthetic but rule-based, generated by `generate_data.py` (seeded, so it rebuilds
exactly the same every time). Period: **Jan 2025 – Jun 2025 (180 days)**.

| Table | Rows | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `products` | 146 | product master, price, category, stock reliability |
| `customers` | 1,200 | city, signup date, segment (new / returning / loyal) |
| `sessions` | 14,000 | one row per app or web visit, with channel and device |
| `funnel_events` | 72,549 | stage log: view → cart → checkout → order |
| `orders` | 4,395 | order value, status, cancel reason, who cancelled |
| `order_items` | 8,162 | line items with item status |
| `substitutions` | 690 | swap offered, price gap, accepted or rejected |

**Total: about 100,000 rows across 7 linked tables.**

## How to run

```bash
git clone <your-repo-url>

# option 1 — sqlite3 shell
sqlite3 funnel.db
.read sql/01_schema.sql
.read sql/02_load_data.sql
.read sql/03_funnel_analysis.sql

# option 2 — python (no sqlite3 shell needed)
python setup_db.py
sqlite3 funnel.db ".read sql/04_cancellation_analysis.sql"
```

To rebuild the CSVs from scratch: `python generate_data.py`

## Main funnel

| Stage | Sessions | Share of visits |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visit started | 14,000 | 100.0% |
| Product viewed | 10,142 | 72.4% |
| Added to cart | 7,367 | 52.6% |
| Checkout started | 5,239 | 37.4% |
| Order placed | 4,395 | 31.4% |
| **Order delivered** | **3,846** | **27.5%** |

The biggest single drop is view → cart (**27.5% of viewers leave**). But the most
expensive drop is the last one: 549 orders die *after* the customer already paid
attention, packed a basket and confirmed. That is the part this project digs into.

## Key findings

### 1. A rejected substitution is the strongest cancel signal in the data

| Order group | Orders | Cancel rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| No swap offered | 3,735 | 9.4% |
| Swap offered and accepted | 383 | 7.8% |
| **Swap offered and rejected** | **277** | **60.3%** |

An offered swap is harmless — accepted swaps cancel *less* than normal orders.
A rejected swap is fatal: **6.4× the baseline cancel rate**.

### 2. Price is what makes customers reject a swap

| Substitute price vs original | Swaps offered | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cheaper or same | 342 | 71.1% |
| Up to ₹50 costlier | 95 | 63.2% |
| More than ₹50 costlier | 253 | **41.5%** |

Overall acceptance is 59.1%. Pushing a costlier replacement drops acceptance by
**29.6 percentage points** — and every rejection carries a 60% chance of losing the whole order.

### 3. Stock problems sit in three fresh categories

| Category | Items ordered | Stock-problem items | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fruits & Vegetables | 1,517 | 365 | 24.1% |
| Dairy & Eggs | 739 | 146 | 19.8% |
| Bakery | 659 | 125 | 19.0% |
| Personal Care | 745 | 29 | 3.9% |

These same three categories top the cancel-rate table (14.0–14.7% vs 8.4% for Personal Care).
Products with stock reliability under 82% show a **14.5%** cancel rate against **10.3%** for reliable products.

### 4. Traffic quality varies 3.6× across channels

| Channel | Visits | Orders | Visit → order |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct | 2,202 | 1,122 | 51.0% |
| Email | 1,735 | 827 | 47.7% |
| Organic | 3,953 | 1,477 | 37.4% |
| Paid ads | 3,570 | 608 | 17.0% |
| Social | 2,540 | 361 | 14.2% |

Paid ads and social bring **43.6% of all visits** but only **22.0% of orders**.
Mobile app converts at 38.4% against 18.9% on mobile web.

### 5. Traffic is concentrated, revenue is not

The **top 10 products out of 146 (6.8%) take 27.0% of all product views**.
A concentrated catalogue means a stock-out on one hero product hits the funnel hard —
which is exactly what finding 3 shows.

---

## What the business should do

- Cap substitute price at or below the original price → recovers roughly **150 of the 277 rejected-swap orders**
- Fix stock reliability on the 12 worst fresh SKUs before spending more on ads
- Move paid-ads and social budget toward email and direct, which convert 3× better
- Ask for swap approval *before* billing, not after — 24.2% of all cancellations name `substitute_rejected`

## Four points for a resume or report

> Use these as-is. Every number comes from `results/query_outputs.txt`.

1. **Built an end-to-end e-commerce funnel analysis in SQL on a 7-table, ~100,000-row relational dataset (14,000 sessions, 72,549 funnel events, 4,395 orders), mapping visit → view → cart → checkout → order and isolating a 68.6% cumulative drop-off across five funnel stages.**

2. **Identified rejected substitutions as the single strongest predictor of order cancellation — 60.3% cancel rate versus a 9.4% baseline (6.4×) — explaining 24.2% of all cancellations and ₹61,797 of the ₹2.73 lakh lost order value.**

3. **Quantified the price sensitivity of substitutions across 690 swap offers: acceptance fell from 71.1% for same-or-cheaper replacements to 41.5% when the substitute cost ₹50+ more, a 29.6 percentage-point drop, and recommended a price-capped swap rule projected to recover ~150 orders.**

4. **Segmented cancellation and conversion by product, category and channel across 146 SKUs, showing that three fresh categories carry 19–24% stock-failure rates and 14%+ cancel rates, while paid and social traffic generated 43.6% of visits but only 22.0% of orders — a 3.6× conversion gap versus direct traffic.**
